Dynamic Ambilight System

A custom ESP32-controlled lighting system that captures video output and changes room LED colours in real time.

The Concept

Commercial Ambilight systems are expensive and locked to specific ecosystems. I built a fully programmable alternative around an ESP32: the screen's edge colours are captured frame by frame and streamed to the board. The ESP32 drives RGB LEDs behind the monitor: whatever colour appears on the screen lights up the same colour on the LEDs, always in sync with the display.

01 — CAPTURE

Screen Capture

A desktop script grabs the screen edges continuously and calculates the dominant RGB value for the top, bottom, left and right zones of every frame.

Edge samplingPer-frame4 zones
02 — TRANSPORT

Data Processing

The colour values are packed into a compact byte stream and pushed through the USB serial port — fast enough that the CPU never notices. Watch the packets travel along the cable.

USB / COMCompact framesZero lag
03 — BRAIN

ESP32 Microcontroller

The dual-core ESP32 decodes the incoming serial stream instantly and maps each colour to its zone of the LED strip.

On the board

Metal-shielded WROOM module with PCB antenna, USB-to-serial bridge, EN and BOOT buttons, and 2×19 pin headers seated on a breadboard.

04 — OUTPUT

Addressable LEDs

The RGB (WS2812B) strip behind the monitor is split into zones. Each LED copies the colour of the screen area in front of it, so the wall glow always matches what is on screen. Check the live RGB readout at the bottom left.
